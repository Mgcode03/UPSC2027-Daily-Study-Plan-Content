# Day 001 – Deep Understanding: Making of the Constitution (Part 2)

## Detailed Working Method of the Constituent Assembly

The Constituent Assembly did not work in a hurry. Every major provision was discussed thoroughly. Members were given the freedom to speak. Debates were recorded and published. This transparency is one reason the Constitution still commands respect.

### Stages of Discussion

1. **Committee Stage**: Various committees prepared reports on specific subjects (Fundamental Rights, Union Powers, Provincial Constitution, etc.).
2. **First Reading**: The Draft Constitution prepared by the Drafting Committee was introduced in the Assembly.
3. **Clause-by-Clause Discussion**: Every article was taken up one by one. Members could move amendments. Ambedkar and other members of the Drafting Committee replied to the points raised.
4. **Second Reading**: After amendments were accepted or rejected, the revised draft was considered again.
5. **Third Reading**: Final reading and adoption.

On 26 November 1949, the motion “that the Constitution as settled by the Assembly be passed” was adopted. Members signed the document over the next few days. The original copies (one in English and one in Hindi) were signed by 284 members.

## Important Features of the Constituent Assembly Debates

The debates reveal the thinking of the founding fathers on critical issues:

### On the Nature of the State
There was clear consensus that India would be a republic (no hereditary ruler) and a democracy based on adult franchise. The idea of a Hindu Rashtra or a theocratic state was firmly rejected. Secularism was accepted as a foundational value even though the word “secular” was added to the Preamble only in 1976.

### On Federalism
The framers wanted a strong Centre because of the painful experience of partition and the need to hold a diverse country together. At the same time, they gave substantial autonomy to the states. The result is often described as a “quasi-federal” or “federal with unitary bias” system.

### On Fundamental Rights vs Directive Principles
There was intense debate on whether Directive Principles should be justiciable (enforceable in court) or not. Finally it was decided that Fundamental Rights would be justiciable and Directive Principles would be non-justiciable but fundamental in the governance of the country. This balance remains important even today.

### On Minorities
Special provisions for minorities (religious and linguistic) were carefully drafted. The Assembly rejected separate electorates (which had been a feature of British rule) and instead provided for joint electorates with reservation of seats for Scheduled Castes and Scheduled Tribes for a limited period.

### On Language
The language question was emotional. Hindi was accepted as the official language of the Union, but English was continued for official purposes for a transitional period. States were given freedom to choose their own official languages.

## Cost and Time

The total expenditure on the Constituent Assembly was approximately Rs 64 lakh. The time taken (almost three years) was considered reasonable by international standards. The American Constitution took about four months, but the Indian document had to deal with a far more complex society and a much larger population.

---

## Enforcement of the Constitution

Some provisions of the Constitution came into force on 26 November 1949 itself (citizenship, elections, provisional Parliament, temporary and transitional provisions). The remaining provisions came into force on 26 January 1950. From that day India became a Republic.

The Constituent Assembly continued as the Provisional Parliament of India until the first general elections were held in 1951-52 and the new Parliament was constituted.

## Criticism of the Constituent Assembly (and the Response)

Critics have pointed out certain limitations:

1. **Not directly elected**: The Assembly was elected by Provincial Assemblies which themselves had limited franchise. Response: In the conditions of 1946, universal adult franchise election for the Assembly was practically impossible. The Assembly still represented all major sections of Indian society.

2. **Congress dominance**: The Congress had an overwhelming majority. Response: The Muslim League initially participated but later withdrew after partition. Other parties and independents were present. The quality of debate remained high.

3. **Borrowed Constitution**: Critics say it is a patchwork. Response: Every modern constitution borrows. The Indian Constitution’s originality lies in the synthesis and in the detailed administrative provisions suited to Indian conditions.

4. **Too lengthy**: It is the longest written constitution. Response: The length was deliberate. The framers wanted to leave as little as possible to future interpretation in a diverse and newly independent country.

Despite these criticisms, the Constitution has proved remarkably resilient. It has been amended more than 100 times, yet its basic structure remains intact.

## Key Personalities and Their Contributions (Beyond Ambedkar)

- **Jawaharlal Nehru**: Moved the Objective Resolution; chaired important committees; gave philosophical direction.
- **Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel**: Chaired the Advisory Committee on Fundamental Rights and Minorities; played a decisive role in the integration of princely states (which was going on simultaneously).
- **Dr. Rajendra Prasad**: Steered the Assembly as President with patience and impartiality.
- **Alladi Krishnaswami Ayyar**: Legal expert who clarified many complex constitutional points.
- **K.M. Munshi**: Contributed significantly to Fundamental Rights and cultural provisions.
- **B.N. Rau**: Constitutional Advisor who prepared the initial draft after studying foreign constitutions.
