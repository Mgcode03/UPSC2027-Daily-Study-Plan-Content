## Historical Background: How India Reached the Point of Making a Constitution

When the British left India in 1947, the country was not starting from zero. Several earlier experiments and documents had already shaped the political thinking of Indian leaders.

### Early Demands for a Constitution

In 1928, the Motilal Nehru Committee (also called the Nehru Report) presented the first major Indian attempt to draft a constitution. It demanded dominion status, fundamental rights, and a parliamentary system. Though the British rejected it, the document planted important ideas.

In 1934, the Congress officially demanded a Constituent Assembly elected on the basis of adult franchise. This demand was repeated many times thereafter.

### The Cabinet Mission Plan (1946)

The actual process began with the Cabinet Mission Plan of 1946. Three British Cabinet Ministers came to India and proposed a scheme for the transfer of power. One of its key features was the formation of a Constituent Assembly to frame the Constitution of free India.

According to the plan:
- The Constituent Assembly would be indirectly elected by the Provincial Legislative Assemblies.
- Seats were allotted in proportion to the population of each province.
- Communities (General, Muslim, Sikh) were given separate representation.
- The Assembly would also work as the Dominion Legislature until the new Constitution came into force.

The elections to the Provincial Assemblies were held in early 1946. The Congress won a large majority of the general seats. The Muslim League won most of the Muslim seats.

### Composition of the Constituent Assembly

The Constituent Assembly of India originally had 389 members:
- 292 members elected from British Indian provinces
- 93 members nominated by the princely states
- 4 members from the Chief Commissioners’ provinces

After the partition of India on 15 August 1947, the number of members from the areas that went to Pakistan was reduced. The final strength of the Constituent Assembly of India became 299.

Important point: The Assembly was not elected by universal adult franchise. It was elected by the limited franchise of the Provincial Assemblies (which themselves had restricted franchise under the Government of India Act 1935). Despite this, the Assembly enjoyed high legitimacy because almost all major political parties and communities were represented.

### First Meeting and Leadership

The first meeting of the Constituent Assembly was held on 9 December 1946 in the Constitution Hall (now Central Hall of Parliament), New Delhi.

Dr. Sachchidananda Sinha was elected as the temporary Chairman (because he was the oldest member).

On 11 December 1946, Dr. Rajendra Prasad was elected as the permanent President of the Constituent Assembly.

H.C. Mukherjee was elected as the Vice-President.

B.N. Rau was appointed as the Constitutional Advisor. He prepared the initial draft based on comparative study of other constitutions.

### The Objective Resolution

On 13 December 1946, Jawaharlal Nehru moved the historic Objective Resolution. This resolution laid down the philosophy and guiding principles of the future Constitution. It declared that India would be an independent sovereign republic, that all power would be derived from the people, that there would be justice (social, economic, political), equality of status and opportunity, and freedom of thought, expression, belief, faith, worship, vocation, association and action.

The Objective Resolution was unanimously adopted on 22 January 1947. Its spirit later found expression in the Preamble of the Constitution.

## Working of the Constituent Assembly

The Constituent Assembly worked for 2 years, 11 months and 18 days. It held 11 sessions. The total number of sitting days was 165.

### Committees of the Assembly

To work efficiently, the Assembly appointed several committees. The most important ones were:

1. **Union Powers Committee** – Chairman: Jawaharlal Nehru
2. **Union Constitution Committee** – Chairman: Jawaharlal Nehru
3. **Provincial Constitution Committee** – Chairman: Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel
4. **Drafting Committee** – Chairman: Dr. B.R. Ambedkar
5. **Advisory Committee on Fundamental Rights, Minorities and Tribal and Excluded Areas** – Chairman: Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel
   - This had several sub-committees, including the Fundamental Rights Sub-Committee (J.B. Kripalani) and the Minorities Sub-Committee (H.C. Mukherjee).
6. **Rules of Procedure Committee** – Chairman: Dr. Rajendra Prasad
7. **States Committee** – Chairman: Jawaharlal Nehru
8. **Steering Committee** – Chairman: Dr. Rajendra Prasad

The Drafting Committee was the most crucial. It consisted of seven members:
- Dr. B.R. Ambedkar (Chairman)
- N. Gopalaswami Ayyangar
- Alladi Krishnaswami Ayyar
- K.M. Munshi
- Syed Mohammad Saadulla
- B.L. Mitter (later replaced by N. Madhava Rau)
- D.P. Khaitan (later replaced by T.T. Krishnamachari)

The Drafting Committee took the reports of various committees and prepared the first draft of the Constitution. This draft was published in February 1948. After receiving comments, a second draft was prepared and presented to the Assembly.

## Important Dates and Timeline (Memorise These)

- 9 December 1946: First meeting of Constituent Assembly
- 11 December 1946: Dr. Rajendra Prasad elected permanent President
- 13 December 1946: Objective Resolution moved by Nehru
- 22 January 1947: Objective Resolution adopted
- 15 August 1947: India becomes independent; Assembly becomes sovereign
- 29 August 1947: Drafting Committee appointed
- 26 November 1949: Constitution adopted (signed by members)
- 24 January 1950: Last meeting of Constituent Assembly; Dr. Rajendra Prasad elected first President of India
- 26 January 1950: Constitution comes into force (Republic Day)

Note: 26 January was chosen because on this day in 1930 the Congress had observed Independence Day (Purna Swaraj resolution of Lahore Session 1929).

## Sources of the Constitution (Conceptual Understanding)

The Indian Constitution is often called a “borrowed” Constitution, but this is only partly true. The framers studied many constitutions and took features that suited Indian conditions. The originality lies in the way these features were blended and adapted.

Key borrowings (you will study these in detail later):
- Parliamentary system, rule of law, single citizenship → Britain
- Fundamental Rights, judicial review, independence of judiciary → USA
- Directive Principles of State Policy → Ireland
- Federation with strong Centre → Canada
- Concurrent List → Australia
- Procedure for amendment → South Africa
- Republic, ideals of liberty, equality, fraternity → France
- Fundamental Duties (later addition) and ideals of justice → USSR (Soviet Union)

The bulk of the Constitution, however, is based on the Government of India Act 1935. Many administrative details, federal structure, emergency provisions, and public service commissions were taken from that Act.

## Role of Dr. B.R. Ambedkar

Dr. Ambedkar is rightly called the Chief Architect of the Indian Constitution. As Chairman of the Drafting Committee, he defended every clause in the Assembly, explained complex provisions in simple language, and guided the debates. His speeches in the Constituent Assembly are still studied for their clarity and depth.

He was not alone. The entire Assembly worked collectively. Nehru, Patel, Rajendra Prasad, Alladi Krishnaswami Ayyar, K.M. Munshi and many others made crucial contributions.
